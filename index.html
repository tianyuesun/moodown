Chapter01

The question that stayed with me

How can we better understand and help the human mind?

This question has stayed with me since adolescence. Every stage of my education and work has been another way of asking it. Select a stage to see what it gave me.

Being able to generate working code is not the same as understanding, maintaining, testing, and engineering a reliable system.

What I do not claim to be

  • An experienced production AI engineer
  • A machine learning researcher
  • A licensed therapist
  • A clinical AI developer

My goal is to become technically capable of building, evaluating, maintaining and eventually commercializing reliable AI systems.

Chapter02

Three problems I want to solve

MoodOwn is a proposed ecosystem of three related products. Each could stand on its own, with its own audience, workflow, commercial strategy and technical difficulty. None of them exists yet. They are concepts I intend to prototype, test and develop.

Technology should support human relationships and professional care. It should never stand in for them.

That principle is shared. Almost everything else differs from one product to the next.

FamilyNeurodivergent and mental health family care

Who it might serveFamilies navigating neurodevelopmental or psychiatric conditions; child and adolescent behavioral health practices; family therapy clinics. A possible entry market is culturally responsive support for East Asian families, without assuming those families are alike.

A child with ADHD struggles with executive functioning. A parent sees laziness or defiance. The same argument repeats until people stop talking.

Communication difficulties like these do not mean the family caused the condition. They do wear a family down. One parent carries most of the caregiving, another underestimates their own part, and a child who feels misunderstood goes quiet.

I picture a condition-aware space where each person can reflect privately, understand what the condition does and does not explain, practice a harder conversation, and share only what they choose. ADHD, autism and mood disorders are different conditions and would need different support.

RelationshipsReflection and care navigation before, and between, therapy

Who it might serveIndividuals and couples seeking relationship support; marriage and family therapists; counseling practices and family therapy clinics.

Many people live with a relationship problem for a long time before they are ready to ask a professional for help.

Someone considering divorce. A teenager who feels alone inside their own family. A couple who cannot talk without escalating. Relatives who disagree about whether therapy is needed at all. These people may not want to confide in friends, and may not know whether they need a counselor, a marriage and family therapist, or someone else. A general-purpose chatbot can be kind, but it has no verified clinical qualifications and no dependable route to a professional.

How can an AI agent help people reflect and reach support without taking control of deeply personal decisions?

ProvidersPersonal care and professional support for healthcare workers

Who it might servePhysicians, nurses, mental health professionals, clinical researchers, residents and trainees; and, as sponsors, healthcare institutions, professional associations and benefits providers.

The people who care for others deserve care that understands their professional world.

This is the product closest to me, and it is not a meditation or burnout app. Clinical expertise does not guarantee access to the right support when a clinician's own life becomes hard. I see four distinct situations.

  • Repeated clinical exposure. Patient after patient whose story resembles a painful part of your own. Each case is manageable. The accumulation may not be.
  • Personal experience and boundaries. Your history makes you more attuned to a patient, and stirs memories. Patients cannot be asked to care for you in return, so you need somewhere separate.
  • Clinicians as patients. Serious illness, postpartum psychiatric symptoms, bereavement, family crisis. Medical knowledge is no immunity.
  • Specialized personal therapy. A therapist with strong skills who also understands supervision, boundaries, ethics and the realities of clinical work. Supervision and personal psychotherapy are different services.

    Chapter04

    Under the surface

    Three separate products can stand on the same engineering foundations while keeping their own safety rules, permissions and clinical considerations. Choose a product to see its rules, and a foundation to see how each product would use it.

    Three products, three sets of agents

    Shared engineering foundations

    I am not proposing one agent that does everything. Each product would have narrowly scoped agents with defined steps and stopping points. What they share is the plumbing: how state is stored, how consent is recorded, how information is retrieved, and how behavior is tested.

    Chapter06

    The next two years

    Four phases of about six months. Each pairs what I plan to study with a more demanding version of MoodOwn, and each is organized around one engineering question. The technologies are learning choices I propose, not skills I already have.

    If the work holds up, a business

    Over time I hope MoodOwn can become a sustainable portfolio of specialized products. That is a direction, not a claim of product-market fit. There are no customers, no revenue and no clinical results to report, so I have not invented any.

    Chapter07

    Why I am returning to computer science

    During my first year studying computer science, I realized that although I enjoyed coding, I still didn't know what I wanted to create with it. So I returned to the question that had started it all.

    Clinical research and my own projects changed how I think about the relationship between psychology and technology. Psychology gives me a sense of purpose, while computer science gives me the skills, confidence, and joy of bringing ideas to life. For me, neither is complete without the other.

    I don't just want to use AI to build products. I want to understand the technology itself and work alongside AI to explore its possibilities and boundaries, including when decisions should be left to humans.

    This time, as I return to computer science, I not only know what I want to create, but also have the confidence that comes from my earlier CS training, rigorous science coursework, and independent research experience. I want to build on that foundation by deepening my knowledge of algorithms, systems design, software architecture, and AI engineering, and to become someone who can help design and build the systems behind the technologies I use.

    For a long time, I thought I was searching for the right direction. Now I realize I was also discovering what I have to offer.
